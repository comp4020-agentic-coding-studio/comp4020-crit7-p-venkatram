# Crit 7 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realising the idea wasn't something to invent for the
brief — it was already in memory. I'd met with someone from ANU Careers
who walked me through almost exactly what ended up on this site: contracts,
tax, super, where to actually look for jobs. At the time none of that lived
in one place; his guidance was scattered advice, not a product. Recognising
that gap gave the prototype a real target instead of a guessed one.

The second thing that moved the work forward was writing with a deliberate
framework in mind rather than just listing facts: Simon Sinek's Why-How-What
structure. Once every page had to answer why something matters before how
and what, the content stopped reading like a reference dump and started
reading like something written for a person who needed convincing to read
it at all.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It sharpened who I want to be: someone who identifies and addresses
problems, not someone who accepts whatever a brief or a generated prompt
hands them. Setting guardrails against fabricated job listings, a literal
Wix Groups integration, and an uncredited success story was that instinct
in practice — noticing the problem in the brief itself and correcting it
before writing any code, rather than shipping what was asked for.

It also left me with two concerns this project doesn't answer. One is
maintaining a product like this once it's shipped, past the version that
looks good in a demo. The other is how to make a product stand out enough
to be worth protecting — patenting an idea, even — when sites can be built
and rebuilt this fast. Speed doesn't remove the question of ownership; it
makes it more urgent.
